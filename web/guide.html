The relay's contacts switch the solenoid's own 12 V supply. Fit the diode across the solenoid coil with its stripe towards +12 V.

The resistor keeps the relay off while the board starts or is being flashed. The diode protects the relay contacts from the coil. Leave neither out. Check the solenoid's current against the relay and supply ratings.

First set-up

  1. Install the firmware on both boards from the install page.
  2. On the controller open Settings and set the date and time.
  3. Open Feeders. Hold the feeder's FLASH button for 3 seconds; its light blinks fast and it appears under Discovered. Press Add.
  4. Open Schedule, press Add feeding time and choose the time, days and portion.
  5. On Home press Feed now to test the mechanism.

A portion is a number of pulses and a pulse length. Start small and watch how much food one pulse drops.

Portions in grams

The feeder has no scale, so it learns grams from one measurement:

  1. Open Feeders and press Calibrate next to the feeder.
  2. Choose the pulse length, hold a cup under the outlet and press Run. The feeder gives 10 pulses.
  3. Weigh the food on a kitchen scale, enter the weight and press Save.

From then on that feeder's portions, daily total, history and SD-card log are in grams. Portions go in steps of one pulse, but you can type any amount in tenths of a gram. The controller rounds to the nearest whole pulse and tells you what you will really get, for example “2.5 g = 4 pulses, gives about 2.4 g”. The amount you asked for is kept with each feeding time, so if you calibrate again the pulses are worked out again and sent to the feeder.

The grams are an estimate. Calibrate again when you change food, and check now and then: pellet size, humidity and how full the hopper is all change what one pulse drops. The calibration run is a normal feed, so the cooldown and daily limit apply to it. Entering 0 removes the calibration.

Optional: a scale under the hopper

With a load cell under the food hopper the feeder weighs what each feed really gave, shows how much food is left, and warns when food runs low or a feed drops next to nothing. Feeders without one keep working from the calibration above.

Mount the hopper so that all of its weight rests on the load cell and nothing else touches it. Then on the controller open Feeders, press Calibrate and Scale:

  1. Empty the hopper and press Zero.
  2. Put a known weight in the hopper (100 g works well), choose it and press Set weight.

A feed is measured as the weight the hopper lost, read about two seconds after the feeder stops. A hopper that is bumped or still swinging at that moment gives no reading for that feed. The scale measures; it does not change how much is fed.

Safety limits

  • No pulse can be longer than 5 seconds, whatever the controller asks for.
  • Cooldown: the shortest time between two feeds. It applies to scheduled feeds too, so keep feeding times further apart than the cooldown.
  • Daily limit: the most relay-on time per day.
  • A feed missed while the feeder had no power is skipped, not run late.

Wi-Fi and internet time

Wi-Fi is optional. On Network press Scan, pick your network and type the password. To type it on a phone instead, press Use phone, join the network shown on the screen (or scan the code) and open http://192.168.4.1. To take the time from the internet, set your UTC offset first, then switch on Internet time. There is no automatic daylight-saving change.

Feed log on an SD card

Put a FAT-formatted SD card in the controller. Every feed and every refused feed is added to feedlog.csv, which opens in any spreadsheet. The History page shows whether a card is in use. Events that happen while no card is inserted are not added later.

Updating

  1. Download the two firmware files from the newest release.
  2. On the controller open Network and note the address under Firmware updates.
  3. In a browser on the same network open that address.
  4. Upload the controller file; the controller restarts.
  5. Upload the feeder file, then press Update next to each feeder.

The update page has no password, so anyone on your network can upload firmware. Use it only on a network you trust.

A feeder is out of contact for about a minute while it updates and skips any feed due in that time. You can also reinstall either board from the install page with a USB cable.
